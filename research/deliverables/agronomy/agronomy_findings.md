Research handoff for the Sheridan 6 prototype — reviewed November 14, 2026.

Delivered three comparison candidates, not recommendations. `supported_candidate` means regional evidence supports including the sequence; it does not establish field suitability. Continuous corn is a provisional baseline until the farmer's actual sequence is known. All local suitability and validated-calendar values remain `null`.

| Candidate | Usable evidence | Why it could be unsuitable |
|---|---|---|
| Continuous corn — supported candidate | Direct limited-irrigation baseline. | Annual volume can look adequate while summer delivery and stored water cannot support the reproductive period. Corn-after-corn also warrants pest review. |
| Corn–grain sorghum — supported candidate | Direct two-year experiment; promising historical economics, without a demonstrated universal yield uplift. | Lower water remaining for following corn, summer demand overlap, maturity/frost exposure, or inadequate sorghum marketing and drying capacity. |
| Corn–winter wheat — conditional | Direct experiment and a different demand season. | Corn harvest can miss wheat establishment; wheat disease and weak wheat-phase returns can outweigh better following-corn performance. |

These judgments use the [2016 full paper](https://acsess.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.2134/agronj2015.0536), the [2021 original report reproduced online](https://www.academia.edu/68865633/Alternative_Cropping_Systems_with_Limited_Irrigation), and the [USDA-hosted abstract of the later economic study](https://www.ars.usda.gov/research/publications/publication/?seqNo115=405022). Source records in the JSON contain verified locators and transfer limits; statements also have source IDs.

The trials do not yield a universal winner. The older experiment favored continuous corn economically but allocated more water to rotated corn. The later study favored corn–sorghum. Different years, treatments, crop sets and economics prevent combining those rankings. Annual results also vary: [the 2017 report](https://newprairiepress.org/kaesrr/vol4/iss8/8/) recorded a sorghum rotation disadvantage and wheat streak mosaic crop failure, while the later aggregate showed no significant sorghum rotation effect. Do not count annual reports from the same experiment as independent trials.

The calendar is deliberately qualified. Year 1 corn precedes wheat sown in autumn of year 1 and harvested in year 2; next corn is year 3. The older trial's September 29 corn harvest/October 1 wheat sowing illustrates the tight turnaround against a [northwest wheat target around September 25](https://extension.k-state.edu/news-and-publications/news/stories/2024/09/agriculture-wheat-planting-considerations.html). Harvest windows and some sowing ranges are [historical statewide NASS observations](https://www.nass.usda.gov/Publications/Todays_Reports/reports/fcdate10.pdf), explicitly including eastern and nonirrigated acreage; they are not local deadlines. Corn–sorghum and continuous corn have overwinter gaps. Wheat-to-corn has a longer summer-through-winter gap, with no extra crop inserted.

Annual allocation, crop ET and peak delivery are separate quantities. The JSON retains the two experiments' irrigation and tillage treatments and the [water-use reference's differing ET tables](https://bookstore.ksre.ksu.edu/pubs/efficient-crop-water-use-in-kansas_MF3066.pdf). Whole-field alternation does not provide the within-year demand spreading of concurrent phase-split acreage. All three crops are grasses: no automatic family-break, nitrogen, disease, carbon or pumping credit is justified. Dead residue protection must also be distinguished from the water consumed by living cover; the [cover-crop source](https://www.ars.usda.gov/ARSUserFiles/30100500/Publications%202010-2019/22PDF%20Water%20Conundrum.pdf) is predominantly dryland/Great Plains evidence, not a Sheridan 6 treatment estimate.
